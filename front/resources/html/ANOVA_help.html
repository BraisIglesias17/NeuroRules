Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

ANOVA, or Analysis of Variance, is a statistical test used to determine if there are any statistically significant differences between the means of three or more independent (unrelated) groups. ANOVA tests the null hypothesis that all group means are equal. Assumptions and Considerations:
  • It's important to check the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variances.
  • ANOVA is sensitive to outliers, so it's essential to inspect the data for extreme values.
  • If assumptions are violated, alternative non-parametric tests like the Kruskal-Wallis test may be considered.

One-way ANOVA is a powerful tool for comparing means across multiple groups.It allows researchers to determine whether there are significant differences among group means and provides valuable information for further post-hoc analysis when necessary.
