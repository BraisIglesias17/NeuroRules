Mann-Whitney U Test

The Mann-Whitney U test is a non-parametric test used to assess whether two independent groups come from the same distribution. It is useful when the assumptions of an independent samples t-test are not satisfied. The test works as follows:
  • Assumption: observations are independent, the outcome is at least ordinal, and the two groups have similarly shaped distributions if the result is interpreted as a median difference.
  • Null hypothesis (H0): a randomly selected observation from either group is equally likely to be larger than an observation from the other group.
  • Ranking: combine the observations from both groups, rank them, and compare the rank totals between the groups.
  • Assigning Ranks: Assign ranks to the absolute differences, starting from 1 for the smallest absolute difference. Ties (i.e., identical absolute differences) receive the average of the ranks they would have gotten.
  • Calculating the Test Statistic (W): Sum the ranks of the positive differences. The test statistic (W) is the smaller The U statistic is calculated from the group rank totals and is used to test the null hypothesis.
  • Determining significance: use the p-value associated with the U statistic. If the p-value is smaller than the chosen significance level (commonly 0.05), you reject the null hypothesis.
In summary, the Mann-Whitney U test compares two independent groups without requiring normality. It is not the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, which is intended for paired observations.
